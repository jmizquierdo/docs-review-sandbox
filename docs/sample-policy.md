# Purpose

This policy defines the rules for protecting company information.
It applies to all employees, contractors and third parties.
The policy is reviewed annually by the Security Office.

Information is one of the company's most valuable assets.
Customers, partners and regulators trust us to keep it confidential, accurate and available when it is needed.
A single incident can damage that trust, interrupt our services and lead to legal penalties.

## Objectives

This policy has four objectives:

- protect the confidentiality of customer, employee and company data;
- keep information accurate and complete, and protect it against unauthorised changes;
- make sure systems and data are available to the people who need them;
- meet the legal, regulatory and contractual obligations that apply to the company.

## Roles and responsibilities

The **Security Office** owns this policy, monitors compliance and coordinates the response to incidents.
**Managers** make sure their teams know the policy and follow it.
**System owners** apply the technical controls described here to the systems they are responsible for.
**Every person** covered by this policy protects the information they handle and reports anything suspicious.

## Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy must be requested in writing to the Security Office.
Each request explains the business need, the risk, and the measures that reduce that risk.
Approved exceptions are recorded, have an expiry date, and are reviewed at least once a year.

# Scope

All information systems owned or operated by the company are in scope.
Personal devices are in scope when they access company data.

## Information covered

The policy covers information in every form: stored in systems, printed on paper, or shared in conversation.
It also covers information that the company holds on behalf of customers and partners.

## Systems covered

The following systems are in scope:

- servers, workstations and laptops provided by the company;
- network equipment, including routers, switches, firewalls and wireless access points;
- cloud services and software-as-a-service applications used for company work;
- mobile phones and tablets, whether provided by the company or personally owned, when they access company email or data.

## Third parties

Contractors and suppliers who access company information must accept this policy before access is granted.
Contracts with suppliers include security requirements that are at least as strict as this policy.
Access for third parties is limited to what the contract needs, and is removed when the contract ends.

# Password requirements

Passwords must have at least 12 characters.
Passwords must be changed every 90 days, or immediately if a compromise is suspected.
Passwords must not be reused across the last 5 changes.
Accounts are locked after 3 failed login attempts.

## Creating a strong password

A long passphrase is easier to remember and harder to guess than a short, complex password.
Use several unrelated words, and avoid names, dates and other personal details that others could find out.
Never use the same password for a company account and a personal account.

## Multi-factor authentication

Multi-factor authentication is required for remote access, email, and every system that holds confidential or restricted data.
Approved second factors are the company authenticator app and hardware security keys.
Text messages are only accepted when no other factor is available, and only with approval from the Security Office.

## Protecting passwords

Passwords must never be shared, written down in plain text, or sent by email or chat.
Store them in the company-approved password manager.
If you think someone knows your password, change it at once and report it as an incident.

## Service accounts

Accounts used by applications instead of people are called service accounts.
Their passwords are generated at random, are at least 24 characters long, and are kept in the company secrets vault.
Every service account has a named owner who reviews its access every six months.

## Classifying information

The person who creates or receives information is responsible for giving it a classification level.
When in doubt, choose the higher level and ask the Security Office.
Classification is reviewed when the content changes, and at least once a year for restricted information.

## Handling rules

**Public** information can be shared with anyone, but only after it has been approved for publication.
**Internal** information can be shared with employees and contractors, but not outside the company.
**Confidential** information is shared only with people who need it for their work, and is encrypted when stored or sent.
**Restricted** information is limited to named people, encrypted at all times, and every access to it is logged.

## Retention and disposal

Information is kept only as long as the law, a contract or a business need requires.
Paper documents classified as confidential or restricted are shredded.
Storage devices are wiped with an approved tool, or physically destroyed, before they are reused or thrown away.

# Incident reporting

Security incidents must be reported within 24 hours.
Reports are sent to the Security Office using the incident form.
Critical incidents must be reported within 4 hours.

## What to report

Report anything that could put company information at risk, even if you are not sure it is an incident.
Examples include a lost laptop or phone, a suspicious email or link, a password shared by mistake, or data sent to the wrong person.

## Critical incidents

An incident is critical when restricted data may have been exposed, when a key service is unavailable, or when there are signs of an active attack.
For critical incidents, call the Security Office hotline as well as sending the incident form.

## After an incident

The Security Office investigates every report and keeps the reporter informed.
Once the incident is closed, a short review records what happened, why, and what will be changed to prevent it from happening again.
Nobody is penalised for reporting an incident in good faith, even if it was caused by their own mistake.
